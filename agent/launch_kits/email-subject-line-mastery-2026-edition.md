Most advice on email subject lines is either vague or trying to sell you something. So here's the actual workflow I use, free:

1. Write the subject line after the email, not before. Pull it from the strongest reader payoff in the body: saved time, avoided risk, clearer decision, faster result, or useful surprise.
2. Use a "one promise only" rule. If the email has three benefits, choose the one most likely to make the reader open now and leave the rest for the preview text or first line.
3. Draft five angles before polishing: direct benefit, curiosity gap, mistake to avoid, timely trigger, and personal note. The best subject usually comes from angle selection, not wordsmithing.
4. Keep curiosity honest. A good curiosity subject creates an information gap the email actually closes; a bad one hides the topic and trains readers not to trust future sends.
5. Test against the inbox scan: sender name + subject + preview text should make sense together in under two seconds. If the preview text repeats the subject, rewrite one of them.
