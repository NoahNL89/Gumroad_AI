**Body (paste, then trim to your voice):**
Most people still treat Gemini like a short-context model — chunking PDFs, pasting excerpts, summarizing *before* they ask questions. With a 1M–10M token window, that leaves almost all of the capability on the table. Here's the workflow I switched to:

1. **Load the whole artifact first, then prompt.** Don't paste snippets. Upload the entire report / codebase / manuscript, and open your prompt with an explicit instruction: *"Do not summarize or sample — process the entire content."* Models default to skimming; you have to tell it not to.
2. **Ask for a structure map + location-anchored evidence, not a summary.** My go-to first pass asks for: a section/chapter map with page ranges, key entities with frequency counts, core claims *with the quote and location of the supporting evidence*, and any internal contradictions. Forcing it to cite locations kills hallucination and makes the output verifiable.
3. **Synthesize across documents in ONE prompt — never separately.** Upload 2–10 files and ask for a unified timeline, an agreement map (where all sources agree, with a citation from each), and a conflict map (where they contradict, with the exact conflict). The whole point of full-context is that a claim in doc 1 that contradicts doc 12 stays *visible* — chunking makes that connection invisible.
4. **Set up a Standing Context "Gem."** Put your company brief, product description, brand voice and target customer into a Gem's system prompt once. Every future chat inherits it — you stop re-pasting your background on every prompt.
